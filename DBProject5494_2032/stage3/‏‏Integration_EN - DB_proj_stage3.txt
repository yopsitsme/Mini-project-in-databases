Combining two ERD schemas into one schema: a system for managing sports clubs in a school

stage 1:
In this stage, the entities Student, Instructor, and Parent were unified by introducing a
generalized super-entity called Person.
This decision was based on the observation that all three entities represent people and
share common attributes such as identification and contact details.
Accordingly:
● An inheritance hierarchy was defined, where Student, Teacher, and Parent inherit
from Person.
● All existing attributes of the original entities were preserved.
● In the Person entity:
○ The attribute id was renamed to personId for semantic clarity.
The attribute name was decomposed into first_name and last_name to
improve normalization.
○ The original name attribute was removed after decomposition.
● The Teacher entity was extended with the attribute speciality, based on the
second schema.
● The Parent entity was defined as a subclass of Person.
● The parenting relationship between Parent and Student was retained to represent
parental relations.
● The table name was standardized from Students to Student.
This approach reduces redundancy, improves normalization, and supports future
extensibility.

stage 2:
In this stage, the Location entity was retained but redefined to represent a school rather
than a generic location.
Accordingly:
● The attribute location_name was renamed to school_name to reflect its updated
semantics.
● The system was defined as school-dependent, with each location representing a
distinct school.
● The Year_Group entity was preserved and is now associated with both
Group_Of_Sports and Location, since age groups are defined within a specific
school context.
● The direct relationship between Location and Sports_Class was removed, as
sports classes are associated with groups rather than directly with schools.
This modification more accurately models the organizational structure of a schoolbased
sports program system.

stage 3:
In the unified schema, the Activity entity—intended to represent general activity types (e.g.,
sports, baking, arts)—was intentionally excluded.
After analyzing system requirements, it was determined that the system would focus
exclusively on sports programs.
Therefore:
● The Activity entity was removed.
● The Sports_Class entity was retained to define specific types of sports classes (e.g.,
basketball, swimming).
This decision simplifies the data model and avoids unnecessary generalization.

stage 4:
In the incoming schema, the entities Weekly_Sessions and Grps served a role similar to
Group_Of_Sports in the other schema.
After consideration, it was decided to:
● Retain Group_Of_Sports as the central entity representing a sports group.
● Convert Weekly_Sessions to Group_Details. Contains all information related to
schedules (day of week, start time, end time, room).
Additionally:
● The attribute max_students was removed from Group_Of_Sports, as capacity
constraints are already defined in Sports_Class via the capacity attribute.
● The attribute group_name was removed, since the group’s identity is derived from
the associated Sports_Class and the group’s level.
This approach improves normalization, reduces redundancy, and clarifies entity
responsibilities.


Note: In their ERD, the 'can enroll' relationship is expressed in the studentsgroups table, and
in ours, the data was transferred in a unified manner to the participate in table.
